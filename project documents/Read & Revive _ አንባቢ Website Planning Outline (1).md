# **1\. Brand Foundation**

## **1.1 Brand Name**

### **Primary Name**

# **Read & Revive**

አንባቢ

# **4\. Vision, Mission, and Values**

## **4.1 Vision**

**To build a generation of young readers in Ethiopia who think deeply, express clearly, grow consistently, and use ideas to create positive change.**

Alternative shorter version:

**To revive a strong reading culture where young people read, reflect, express, and act.**

## **4.2 Mission**

**To make reading accessible, engaging, and rewarding for young people in Ethiopia through organized book cycles, peer-based discussions, digital content, and youth-generated reflections.**

## **4.3 Core Values**

### **1\. Consistency**

Reading culture is not built in one day. The platform should help readers build small but repeated habits.

**Brand meaning:**  
We celebrate daily progress, not only final completion.

### **2\. Reflection**

Reading should not be passive. Readers should think, question, connect, and respond.

**Brand meaning:**  
Every book should lead to ideas, conversations, and personal insight.

### **3\. Community**

Reading should not feel lonely. A circle makes reading easier, warmer, and more motivating.

**Brand meaning:**  
We make books social without making them shallow.

### **4\. Expression**

Young people should not only consume books. They should produce ideas from what they read.

**Brand meaning:**  
Reflections, articles, discussions, and reader voices are part of the platform’s identity.

### **5\. Growth**

Books should help young people become more aware, confident, disciplined, and purposeful.

**Brand meaning:**  
Reading is connected to character, mindset, and real-life improvement.

### **6\. Recognition**

Effort should be seen. Readers should feel that their consistency, reflections, and contributions matter.

**Brand meaning:**  
Badges, streaks, leaderboards, certificates, and featured voices should reward meaningful engagement, not empty activity.

### **7\. Impact**

Reading should move beyond the page.

**Brand meaning:**  
The final goal is not only finishing books. It is building people who think better, speak better, write better, and contribute better.

# **6\. Audience Pain Points**

## **6.1 Main Problem**

**Many young people in Ethiopia struggle to build consistent reading habits because reading often feels inaccessible, lonely, irrelevant, or unrewarding.**

## **6.2 Root Causes**

| Root Cause | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Limited access to appealing books | Readers do not always know what to read or where to begin |
| Lack of structure | No clear timeline, target, or accountability |
| Reading feels lonely | No circle, discussion, or shared motivation |
| Digital distraction | Entertainment easily replaces reading |
| Content feels disconnected | Many books do not feel related to local life, youth identity, or personal growth |
| No reward system | Readers do not feel recognized for effort |
| Weak writing culture | Young readers are rarely encouraged to turn reading into expression |
| Low community accountability | People start books but do not finish them |

## **6.3 Emotional Problem**

The emotional problem is deeper than “people do not read.”

Many young people feel:

* “I want to read, but I cannot stay consistent.”  
* “I start books but do not finish.”  
* “Reading feels boring alone.”  
* “I do not know what to read.”  
* “I want people to discuss ideas with.”  
* “I want my thoughts to matter.”  
* “I want reading to change something in me.”

Read & Revive should speak to this emotional layer.

# **14\. Product Philosophy**

This is important because you are building a long-term system.

## **14.1 The Platform Should Not Only Inform**

Most websites only tell users:

Who we are  
What we do  
Contact us

Read & Revive should do more.

It should make the user feel:

I can join now.  
I can start reading now.  
I can track my progress.  
I can belong to a circle.  
I can be recognized.  
My ideas can be published.

---

## **14.2 Every Feature Must Support One of Five Goals**

| Goal | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Start** | Help users join easily |
| **Continue** | Help users stay consistent |
| **Reflect** | Help users think deeply |
| **Connect** | Help users discuss with others |
| **Contribute** | Help users publish and inspire others |

Any future feature should be tested against these five goals.

If a feature does not help users start, continue, reflect, connect, or contribute, it may not belong.

# **15\. Gamification Philosophy**

Detailed badge and streak rules will come in Document 3, but the brand philosophy must be locked now.

## **15.1 Gamification Purpose**

Gamification is not decoration.

It should help readers:

* Stay consistent  
* Feel progress  
* Return daily  
* Join discussions  
* Complete books  
* Write reflections  
* Encourage others  
* Build identity as readers

---

## **15.2 What Gamification Should Reward**

Read & Revive should reward meaningful actions.

Reward:

| Action | Why it matters |
| ----- | ----- |
| Daily reading | Builds habit |
| Reflection | Builds thinking |
| Discussion | Builds community |
| Completion | Builds discipline |
| Writing | Builds expression |
| Encouragement | Builds healthy culture |
| Consistency over time | Builds identity |

---

## **15.3 What Gamification Should Not Reward Too Much**

Avoid rewarding only:

* Random clicks  
* Empty comments  
* Spam activity  
* Logging in without reading  
* Competing aggressively  
* Quantity without quality

The platform should not become a shallow points game.

The system should feel like:

**Progress with purpose.**

Not:

**Points for everything.**

---

## **15.4 Long-Term Gamification Principle**

The system should be built with layers:

Layer 1: Participation  
Layer 2: Consistency  
Layer 3: Reflection  
Layer 4: Community contribution  
Layer 5: Publishing and leadership  
Layer 6: Long-term reader identity

This means badges should not only be:

Day 1  
Day 3  
Day 7

They should also recognize:

* Thoughtful reflections  
* Helping others  
* Completing difficult books  
* Leading discussions  
* Publishing articles  
* Returning across multiple cycles  
* Inviting new readers  
* Building community culture

* Reviewer/expert closing sessions

* Fake testimonials  
* Random stock names

# **21\. Brand Experience Principles**

Every page, feature, and post should follow these principles.

## **Principle 1: Make reading feel social**

Do not present reading as lonely self-improvement only. Show circles, discussions, and shared progress.

---

## **Principle 2: Make progress visible**

Users should see timelines, streaks, milestones, completion, and next steps.

---

## **Principle 3: Make reflection central**

The platform should not only ask, “Did you read?”

It should also ask:

What did you think?  
What changed in you?  
What question stayed with you?  
What idea should others hear?

---

## **Principle 4: Make the next action clear**

Every page should guide the user toward one clear next step.

Examples:

Join Current Reading Cycle  
Read the latest reflection  
Explore upcoming programs  
Submit your article  
Join the community

---

## **Principle 5: Make the brand feel alive**

The website should always show signs of activity:

* Current book  
* Active timeline  
* Upcoming discussion  
* New blog posts  
* Reader progress  
* Community highlights  
* Weekly prompts

A dead-looking website will weaken the movement.

# **23\. Brand Do and Do Not Rules**

## **23.1 Do**

* Do make the brand feel active and alive.  
* Do show reading as a shared journey.  
* Do use playful language with purpose.  
* Do connect books to real life.  
* Do celebrate consistency.  
* Do highlight youth voices.  
* Do make the current reading cycle visible.  
* Do use badges, progress, and challenges meaningfully.

* Do not use fake-looking testimonials.

* Do not use rigid corporate layouts.
