# **Document 5: Content & Editorial Guide**

## **Read & Revive / አንባቢ**

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft for locking  
**Purpose:** This document defines how all written content on Read & Revive should be created, reviewed, published, and connected to the reading movement. It covers book descriptions, reading cycles, reflections, blog articles, tone, categories, publishing rules, and editorial quality.

---

# **1\. Content Vision**

Read & Revive content should not feel like random blog posts or formal organization reports.

The content should feel like:

**Ideas born from books, shaped by discussion, and written by youth voices.**

The website content must support the full movement:

Read → Reflect → Discuss → Write → Publish → Inspire

Every content piece should connect to at least one of these goals:

| Goal | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| **Invite** | Help new readers join |
| **Guide** | Help readers follow the cycle |
| **Reflect** | Help readers think deeply |
| **Discuss** | Help the community talk |
| **Publish** | Turn youth thoughts into articles |
| **Inspire** | Motivate others to read and grow |

---

# **2\. Content Identity**

## **2.1 Main Content Brand**

The blog/content section should be called:

# **Voices from the Circle**

This is stronger than simply calling it “Blog.”

It tells users:

* These writings come from readers.  
* These writings are connected to book circles.  
* The platform gives youth a voice.  
* Reading does not end after finishing the book.

---

## **2.2 Content Promise**

**Read & Revive publishes reflections, articles, and ideas that grow from reading cycles, community discussions, and youth experiences.**

The content is not random. It should always feel connected to:

* Books  
* Reading cycles  
* Reflection prompts  
* Discussion sessions  
* Youth growth  
* Community learning

# **6\. Voice and Tone**

## **6.1 Brand Writing Tone**

Read & Revive content should sound:

* Youthful  
* Clear  
* Warm  
* Reflective  
* Energetic  
* Honest  
* Encouraging  
* Purposeful

It should not sound:

* Too academic  
* Too corporate  
* Too religious-heavy on public pages  
* Too childish  
* Too motivational without depth  
* Too AI-generated

---

## **6.2 Public Tone Example**

### **Avoid**

Our initiative aims to enhance youth intellectual development through strategic content engagement and community-based knowledge-sharing interventions.

### **Use**

Reading becomes easier when you do it with a circle. Read & Revive helps young people follow a clear book timeline, discuss ideas, and turn reflections into voices others can learn from.

---

## **6.3 Blog Tone Example**

### **Avoid**

This book is very important because it has many lessons and it teaches us a lot about life.

### **Use**

The strongest lesson I took from this book is that change does not always begin with big motivation. Sometimes it begins with one small action repeated honestly.

Why we selected this book

We selected \[Book Title\] because it speaks to \[main theme\]. It helps readers think about \[idea 1\], \[idea 2\], and \[idea 3\]. This cycle is for young people who want to \[practical benefit\] and discuss how these ideas connect to real life.

## **8.3 Reading Timeline Format**

| Week | Target | Community Activity |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Week 1 | Chapters 1–3 | First reflection prompt |
| Week 2 | Chapters 4–6 | Group discussion |
| Week 3 | Chapters 7–End | Final reflection \+ closing session |

| Day | Target | Prompt |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |

| Day 2 | Pages 16–30 | What did this section challenge in you? |

# **9\. Reflection Prompt Guide**

Reflection prompts are very important because they turn reading into thinking.

## **9.1 Prompt Types**

| Prompt Type | Purpose | Example |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Personal connection | Connect book to life | “Where have you seen this idea in your own life?” |
| Key idea | Understand the book | “What was the strongest idea in today’s reading?” |
| Question | Encourage curiosity | “What question did this chapter leave in your mind?” |
| Application | Move from idea to action | “What small action can you try from this idea?” |
| Quote reflection | Deepen engagement | “Choose one quote and explain why it matters.” |
| Community discussion | Start conversation | “Do you agree with the author here? Why?” |

---

## **9.2 Daily Reflection Prompt Examples**

* What idea stayed with you today?  
* What sentence would you highlight from today’s pages?  
* What did today’s reading make you question?  
* What is one thing you want to apply?  
* Which idea felt closest to your life?  
* What did you disagree with, and why?  
* How would you explain today’s idea to a friend?

---

## **9.3 Final Reflection Prompt Examples**

* What is the biggest lesson you took from this book?  
* What changed in the way you think?  
* Which idea should more young people hear?  
* What action will you take after this book?  
* What part of the book challenged you most?  
* Would you recommend this book? Why?

# **10\. Article Submission Rules**

## **10.1 Article Types Accepted**

Read & Revive should accept:

* Book-inspired reflections  
* Personal essays  
* Short opinion articles  
* Community discussion responses  
* Book reviews  
* Lessons learned from a cycle  
* “From reflection to action” articles  
* Closing session summaries

---

## **10.2 Article Length**

Recommended length:

| Type | Length |
| ----- | ----- |
| Short reflection | 150–300 words |
| Standard article | 600–1,000 words |
| Deep article | 1,200–1,800 words |
| Book review | 700–1,200 words |
| Session recap | 500–900 words |

For MVP, encourage **600–1,000 words** for most published articles.

---

## **10.3 Article Structure**

A good article should include:

1. **Title**  
   Clear, specific, attractive.  
2. **Opening hook**  
   A personal moment, question, or strong idea.  
3. **Connection to the book**  
   Mention the book, chapter, idea, or discussion.  
4. **Main reflection**  
   Explain the idea clearly.  
5. **Real-life connection**  
   Show why it matters to youth, community, or personal growth.  
6. **Closing thought**  
   End with insight, question, or action.

---

## **10.4 Article Quality Checklist**

Before publishing, every article should pass this checklist.

| Requirement | Yes/No |
| ----- | ----- |
| Connected to a book, cycle, or discussion |  |
| Has a clear main idea |  |
| Uses original reflection |  |
| Not copied from internet or AI |  |
| Clear and respectful language |  |
| Has useful value for readers |  |
| Has proper title |  |
| Has clean grammar after editing |  |
| Does not attack people or groups |  |
| Fits Read & Revive tone |  |

# **11\. Editorial Review Workflow**

## **11.1 Publishing Flow**

Reader submits article  
→ Editor/Admin receives submission  
→ Initial quality check  
→ Grammar and structure editing  
→ Feedback sent to writer if needed  
→ Writer approves final version  
→ Article is scheduled or published  
→ Writer receives recognition  
→ Article appears in Voices from the Circle

---

## **11.2 Article Statuses**

| Status | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Draft | Writer is still preparing |
| Submitted | Sent to the team |
| Under Review | Editor is checking |
| Needs Revision | Writer should improve |
| Approved | Ready for publishing |
| Scheduled | Publishing date selected |
| Published | Live on website |
| Featured | Highlighted on homepage/blog |
| Rejected | Not accepted |
| Archived | Removed from active display |

---

## **11.3 Editing Rule**

Editors may fix:

* Grammar  
* Clarity  
* Structure  
* Repetition  
* Title strength  
* Paragraph flow

Editors should not change the writer’s main idea without approval.

---

# **12\. AI-Generated Content Policy**

Since you do not want the platform to feel AI-generated, this must be controlled.

## **12.1 Rule**

Articles should be based on **real reader reflection**, not generic AI writing.

AI may be used for:

* Grammar support  
* Structure improvement  
* Title suggestions  
* Shortening or polishing

AI should not replace:

* Personal experience  
* Real thought  
* Book connection  
* Original voice

---

## **12.2 Red Flags**

Reject or revise articles that sound like:

* Generic motivational speech  
* No personal insight  
* No book connection  
* Repeated common phrases  
* Too perfect but empty  
* Looks copied from summaries online

# **16\. Content Anti-Rules**

## **Avoid**

* Long NGO-style explanations  
* Random articles not connected to reading  
* Fake testimonials  
* Generic AI-looking posts  
* Overly academic book summaries  
* Too many quotes with no reflection  
* Religious-heavy public messaging

* Clear book connections  
* Youthful but thoughtful language  
* Short powerful paragraphs  
* Attractive article titles  
* Community-generated ideas  
* Strong editing before publishing

# **5\. Community Rules**

These rules should be pinned in Telegram/Facebook.

## **5.1 Public Rules**

1. **Respect every reader.**  
   Discuss ideas, not personalities.  
2. **Stay connected to the reading cycle.**  
   Keep main discussions related to the selected book and prompts.  
3. **No mocking slow readers.**  
   Everyone builds habits at a different pace.  
4. **Share real reflections.**  
   Avoid copy-paste answers or empty comments.  
5. **Encourage others.**  
   A strong circle helps members continue.  
6. **No spam or unrelated promotion.**  
   Links, ads, and unrelated content may be removed.  
7. **Disagree respectfully.**  
   Thoughtful disagreement is welcome. Insults are not.  
8. **Protect privacy.**  
   Do not share private messages or personal information without permission.

# **9\. Discussion Management**

## **9.2 Discussion Rules for Moderators**

Moderators should:

* Start with a clear question  
* Keep the discussion on topic  
* Tag quiet members occasionally, but not pressure them  
* Summarize long discussions  
* Appreciate thoughtful answers  
* Stop disrespectful arguments early  
* Move unrelated topics aside politely

---

## **9.3 Good Discussion Prompt Examples**

* Which idea from this chapter challenged you most?  
* What part of this book feels relevant to youth in Ethiopia?  
* What did you agree with strongly?  
* What did you disagree with?  
* What would change if young people applied this idea?  
* Which idea should become an article?  
* What action can we take from this week’s reading?

# **10\. Moderation System**

## **10.1 Moderation Philosophy**

Moderation should protect the circle without making the community feel controlled.

The tone should be:

* Respectful  
* Calm  
* Clear  
* Fair  
* Encouraging

---

## **10.2 Content That Should Be Removed**

Remove or hide:

* Spam  
* Ads  
* Insults  
* Personal attacks  
* Hate speech  
* Unrelated political fights  
* Repeated copy-paste messages  
* Private information shared without permission  
* Links that may be harmful  
* Content that derails the reading cycle repeatedly

---

## **10.3 Moderation Steps**

| Step | Action |
| ----- | ----- |
| 1 | Friendly reminder |
| 2 | Direct warning |
| 3 | Remove message |
| 4 | Temporary mute |
| 5 | Remove from group if repeated |

---

## **10.4 Moderator Message Template**

Hi, let’s keep this discussion connected to the reading cycle and respectful for everyone. You are welcome to share your idea, but please avoid personal attacks or unrelated topics.

# **17\. Community Health Rules**

A healthy community should be:

* Active but not noisy  
* Friendly but serious  
* Encouraging but not fake  
* Open but moderated  
* Competitive but not stressful  
* Reflective but not boring

## **17.1 Warning Signs**

The community may need intervention if:

* Few people respond to prompts  
* Only admins are posting  
* Discussions become unrelated  
* Members feel shy to share  
* Too many low-quality messages appear  
* Competition becomes unhealthy  
* Readers stop after the first week

---

## **17.2 Fixes**

| Problem | Fix |
| ----- | ----- |
| Low engagement | Use easier prompts and polls |
| Few reflections | Share examples of good reflections |
| Too much noise | Create clear discussion threads |
| People falling behind | Post catch-up encouragement |
| Too formal | Use warmer, shorter messages |
| Same people dominate | Invite quieter members gently |
| No article submissions | Invite selected reflection writers directly |
